# 1 sheep 2 sheep 3 sheep

A small pixel-art psychological story about a kid with insomnia, a counting
ritual, and the one sheep that doesn't belong.

> a quiet story about losing someone.

You can't sleep. The rule is simple: count one hundred sheep. But the count
has scars in it, the dream on the other side of 100 is a fluorescent pasture
that goes on forever, and lately the sheep have started showing up at school.
Nobody else says anything about the sheep. Those are different things — seeing
and saying — and both are worse.

And something lives in the creek at the edge of the dream. It knows the rules
of hide-and-seek better than you do. When it finishes counting, run.

**Playtime:** ~12–15 minutes · one sitting recommended · sound on if you can
(fully playable muted)

## Play

Open `index.html` in any browser. That's it — one file, no build, no assets,
no dependencies. Works from disk or any static host (GitHub Pages, itch.io).

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Arrows / WASD | walk |
| Z / Space / Enter / tap | count · examine · advance text |
| X | (you'll know when) |
| M | mute (persists) |

## What's inside

- One self-contained HTML file (~120 KB). Canvas 2D at 320×180, integer-scaled
  with `image-rendering: pixelated`.
- All art drawn procedurally — a 5×7 bitmap font, three-tone shaded sprites
  with contact shadows, dithered sky gradients, film grain, volumetric light
  shafts, drifting fog, and palette-lerped scenes.
- All audio synthesized live with WebAudio — counting blips, detuned-saw bleats,
  fluorescent hum, a heartbeat that tightens, one splash used exactly twice.
- A 100-count escalation table where no two stretches of the ritual play the
  same: the stare at 13, the miscount at 22, the skip at 43, the field at 50,
  the freeze at 66, the heads at 77, the jam at 88, the stall at 99.
- Letterboxed cutscenes, a hallway where the lights die behind you, a long
  blink with something standing in it, and one chase you will not forget:
  auto-run, one button, jump the fallen fences. It is faster than you.
  It is not faster than the door.

## Content note

This game deals honestly with grief and the loss of a friend, and it has
grown teeth: a stalking creature made of static, one chase sequence, and a
handful of dread-heavy cutscenes. No gore — the horror is the quiet kind,
until it stands up. The red scarf is still the only red in the game.
